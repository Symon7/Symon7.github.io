Research and Statistical Projects

This page highlights selected research and applied statistical projects involving financial data, portfolio selection, statistical modeling, and computational methods.

1. Optimal Portfolio Selection Using Mixture-Design Experiments

This project investigates the application of mixture-design experiments to optimal portfolio selection. The objective is to identify portfolio allocations that achieve desirable investment characteristics, particularly low volatility, while satisfying the constraints on portfolio weights.

Methods and techniques:

  • Space-filling mixture designs
  • Simplex-lattice designs
  • Random simplex sampling
  • Logratio transformations
  • Statistical analysis of portfolio performance

Tools: SAS, R

Related publication: Low Volatility Optimal Portfolio Selection: Financial Experiments with Mixture Designs (2026) .

2. Performance Evaluation of Leveraged Exchange-Traded Funds

This project examines whether leveraged exchange-traded funds achieve their stated leverage objectives relative to their underlying benchmark. The analysis uses daily, weekly, and monthly financial return data.

Methods and techniques:

  • Calculation and comparison of daily, weekly, and monthly returns
  • Leverage ratio assessment
  • Regression analysis
  • Standard deviation and correlation analysis
  • Descriptive statistics, including mean, minimum, maximum, quartiles, skewness, and kurtosis
  • Histogram and boxplot analysis
  • Jackknife and bootstrap resampling techniques

Tools: SAS

3. Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Differential Equations

This research examines the performance of activation functions in Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) for solving autonomous and non-autonomous differential equations.

Research focus:

  • Evaluation of activation functions
  • Solutions to autonomous differential equations
  • Solutions to non-autonomous differential equations
  • Assessment of the implications of activation-function choices for PINN performance
