Research

My research focuses on the application of statistical methods to financial problems, particularly optimal portfolio selection and the evaluation of investment performance.

Research Interests

  • Statistical Finance
  • Optimal Portfolio Selection and Optimization
  • Financial Data Analytics
  • Mixture-Design Experiments
  • Leveraged Exchange-Traded Fund Performance Evaluation
  • Statistical Modeling and Data Science

Current Research

Optimal Portfolio Selection Using Mixture-Design Experiments

My research investigates the use of mixture-design experiments for selecting portfolios under the constraint that portfolio weights are nonnegative and sum to one. I study experimental design strategies, including space-filling mixture designs and simplex-lattice designs, to evaluate portfolio performance and identify low-volatility investment allocations.

This work uses financial market data and statistical analysis to investigate how experimental design methods can support portfolio selection.

Performance Evaluation of Leveraged Exchange-Traded Funds

My doctoral research also examines the performance of leveraged exchange-traded funds. I evaluate whether these funds achieve their stated leverage objectives using daily, weekly, and monthly return data and comparisons with their underlying benchmark.

The analysis includes leverage ratios, regression, standard deviations, correlations, and descriptive statistical measures. I also examine distributional characteristics using skewness, kurtosis, quartiles, histograms, and boxplots. Resampling techniques, including jackknife and bootstrap methods, are considered in the statistical evaluation.

Research Methods and Tools

My research uses statistical modeling, regression analysis, experimental design, descriptive statistics, and financial data analysis. I primarily work with SAS, R, and Python.
